The work behind the output

A model decides what belongs together and how to explain it. Lamina manages the inputs, dependencies and saved results around those decisions.

01

Divide by the task

Small inputs can go straight to a writer. Larger collections go through reading and planning. An agent can also supply its own sections and source assignments.

Workflow options ↗
02

Give workers the context they need

Writers follow one plan, with their section’s sources, nearby section summaries and chosen shared context. In a custom workflow, a job can wait for another job’s completed output.

Dependencies and worker inputs ↗
03

Keep useful work

Completed requests are cached. Revise a section or resume an interrupted project: unchanged requests reuse their saved results, while affected work runs again.

Caching and revision ↗
04

Check the finished result

Review compares each draft with its sources. Practice questions get a blind solve and a marking check. Reports retain unresolved findings and work left out.

Quality evaluation ↗

Parallel where it helps.

Reading can run across sources. Writing and review proceed section by section under a shared call limit. Speech synthesis uses its own resource lane.

When may a retried worker publish?

A lease lets a worker hold a job temporarily. A fencing token distinguishes the current owner from a worker whose lease has expired.

Before you run a project

The public site contains examples; your files and model connection belong in the local app. Generated material needs review. Audio requires a speech adapter, and live oral-exam hosting belongs in a separate application. Model quality, cost and speed depend on the task and provider; the published benchmarks describe their test workloads.
